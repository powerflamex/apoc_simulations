What works:
- One repeatable formula: a single rule that keeps escalating ("...and couldn't stop?") with a big live counter.
- Thumbnail = the counter itself: one huge number + a comparison anyone gets ("hotter than the Sun", "colder than space").
- "You" beats "Earth": You-hotter 79K vs Earth-hotter 49K (same 9,900°F number).
- Hot, bright, busy pictures beat cold, dark, empty ones (the two cold ones and the dim Moon one are lowest).
- Collabs: the 5 posted as a collab with the channel Suit Breach are the top 5; the 2 without are the bottom 2.
  Likely the biggest single factor (they reach Suit Breach's audience too).

What doesn't:
- The Moon one (a proven Shorts topic) did worst: dim purple thumbnail, a distance number with no feel to it, no collab.
- Leaving the niche: the list video got 1.2K.

Not checked: watch time/retention (not public), the videos' insides.

## Hooks (first 35 s of the 2 best and 2 worst, checked frame by frame)

No voiceover in any of them: music only, plus italic subtitles at the bottom and a counter in the top-left
(big number, small lines under it: metric value, place, "AND STILL RISING", a comparison in orange). 16:9, 7 min.

- **You got hotter (79K):** 0-4 s flash-forward to the ending (a glowing spiral like a galaxy) with
  "In 6 minutes, you're hotter than a supernova." Then a busy Santa Monica beach, title at ~6 s, counter already
  climbing (98.6 → 101 → 110°F in the first 10 s). By 16 s, first-person: your own hands up in front of you.
  "The highest fever anyone ever survived: 115.7°F." / "You blow right past it." (a real record, beaten in seconds)
- **Earth spun faster (67K):** 0-4 s flash-forward: Earth squashed into a disc inside a ring of rubble,
  "In 7 minutes, this is Earth." Then a crowded equator beach, title, counter (ONE DAY LASTS 24 h 00 m, ticking down).
  "The equator. 1 PM. A normal day." / "Then Earth starts spinning a little faster." / "A day is only an hour
  shorter. That's enough." (the beach is already emptying at 30 s)
- **Earth got colder (17K, no collab):** no flash-forward. Opens on a calm normal beach, title at 1 s, "3 PM in
  Santa Monica. 72°F." First visible change (the sea freezing) only at ~16 s.
- **Moon got closer (14K, no collab):** has the flash-forward ("In 5 minutes, this is the Moon.") but the picture
  is an unreadable white fuzzy blob in a hazy pastel sky. Then a tiny Moon dot over a dark, empty sea, a distance
  counter nobody can feel, "Every night it rises a little bigger." / "Down on the beach, nobody has noticed yet."
  Nothing visible happens for 30 s; no people.

Takeaways: (1) the two hits open with a 4 s flash-forward of the most extreme moment plus a promise with a time
("In 6 minutes, ..."), so the viewer knows the payoff and that it's coming; (2) the image in that flash-forward must
be clear and striking (the Moon's fuzzy blob wasted it); (3) the counter moves visibly from second one and beats a
real-world record within ~15 s; (4) "you" through first-person hands; (5) crowds and a sunny beach, not empty sea.
Matches our Shorts rules (frame 0 already strange and readable; calm normal-day openings stall).
