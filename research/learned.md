## Why the scores differ (Oct 25 read of the stats + the first 6 s of each video)
- **The question decides it, not the picture.** Trillion-years and big-crunch share the scene and opening shots: score
  56 vs 38. The four winners (black hole passes Earth, your speed doubles, you live 1 trillion years, every woman vanishes)
  need zero science knowledge and make you wonder what happens to *you*. The recent stalls need knowledge or explaining
  first (false vacuum decay, the Big Crunch, Jupiter has no ground, friction holds bolts, oxygen is in water and glass).
  Test for every topic: could a 10-year-old get the question in one second, and does it make them wonder what happens to them?
- **Hits did not open on action:** black hole, trillion and speed all open on a calm city at dusk. "More action at the
  start" is not the fix; the question is.
- **Repeating a formula decays:** doubling: speed 55 -> gravity 48 -> jump 39; time-lapse: trillion 56 -> big crunch 38.
  The first viewers of a new Short are recent viewers, who have already seen the trick.
- Momentum (a smaller first test) explains fewer views on newer Shorts, not their lower scores.
- (Checked Oct 25 and wrong: frame brightness/contrast does not predict stay; see the tsunami note below.)
- Red finish-line badge and gold milestone popups don't decide a hit: trillion-years had both and won; black hole and
  speed-doubles had neither. Keep the badge. Popups must say what is happening ("EVEREST GOES UNDER"), never repeat the
  counter or show a different number from it (found Oct 25 in rain 38 vs 28 ft, sun 1,300x vs 1,000x, quake 13.1 vs 13,
  grow-trillion repeating 10 BILLION x).
- Clutter: mid-video up to 7 text items at once; the tiny lines under the counter can't be read on a phone. Rule: at most
  ~4 text items, one readable line under the counter, never a popup and a caption at the same time. (Cleanup of the five
  unposted Shorts handed to a separate session Oct 25.) Cleaner text won't fix a weak question, but it can't hurt.
- Posting order suggested Oct 25 for the five finished Shorts: tsunami-nyc, sun-explodes, grow-trillion, rain-1000-years,
  quake-grows (most universal question first; formula repeats last).

## Keeping people watching to the end (from the Oct 14-15 stats; apply to every new video)
- The two hits (black hole, speed-doubles) kept ~78-79% of the video watched; later Shorts 64-71%. The openings work
  ("stayed to watch" is the same); the middles lose people. So build every video as ONE rule that gets worse every few
  seconds, with the biggest moment at the very end (not a big event early followed by aftermath).
- Put a finish line on screen from frame 0 that the counter races toward (speed-doubles: "light speed in 53 s";
  gravity-doubles: a red badge "EARTH CAVES IN AT 6,000x" under the counter).
  **But no red badge box (owner, Oct 25: "kinda ass"):** put the finish line in the title, a caption or the counter's
  own text, never as the red box under the counter. Removed from grow-trillion, sun-explodes, quake-grows, rain, tsunami.
- On-screen text (owner, Oct 25): at most ~4 text items at once. One short line under the counter (44px) or none;
  gold popups say what is happening in a phrase, never a number (they contradicted the counter); a popup and a caption
  are never on screen together (captions go in the gaps). Pattern: `say` on each beat, `popupAt(v)`/`POP` in timeline.js.
- Give the escalation a beat: gravity-doubles pops a big gold "4x", "8x"... in the lower middle at each doubling,
  with a camera jolt and a deep boom in the sound.
- Never let the middle sit still: if the story leaves "you" (e.g. you die), move the camera somewhere new (gravity-doubles
  flies out over the river into the burning city).
- Owner rule (Oct 15): don't reuse an old project's scene without asking him first. Shared building blocks
  (crowd.js, cars.js, smoke.js, compose/render code) are fine.

## The owner's feedback on the videos (apply to every new one)
- **Show it, don't just say it.** If a caption claims something (nurses gone, fires, collapse, "more than every nuke"),
  the picture must show it: cutaways (a hospital ward, a classroom), damage, fires, people. A plain time-lapse of an
  empty city was called boring.
- **Effects must be physically believable and connected.** Meteors that appear out of nowhere were rejected: they must be
  visible pieces of the rubble dropping out. Rocks that vanish on landing look wrong: leave a fire/trail into the ground.
  Hits right next to a city with no damage look wrong.
- **Escalate, don't peak early.** Make each hit/event closer or bigger than the last (moon-falls: horizon -> islands ->
  bay -> towers -> half a mile away), so there's always something new to destroy.
- **The first 15 s must change constantly.** "Nothing changes for 15 s" was the main complaint about the first moon cut.
- **Crowds:** people must never jump, teleport or change size. Plan every path up front so nobody overlaps
  (women-vanish/src/people.js) instead of pushing people apart each frame (that caused the jumping in electrons).
  Keep the scene at real scale (Shibuya scramble ~44 x 40 m).
- **Squeaky, glitchy screams (found Oct 29):** the synth crowd screams in earth-stops, friction, jump-doubles, tsunami-nyc,
  punch-10x and black-hole-appears all advanced ONE shared pitch counter (`ph[n] += c.f / SR` inside the cries loop), so
  overlapping voices added their pitches into one high, jumping squeak. Every voice, tap or thud needs its own phase and
  its own filters (`c.ph`, `c.fA`/`c.fB`), as fixed in punch-10x and black-hole-appears (copy those).
- **Sound:** no harsh clicks/clacks/crackles. The owner noticed "plastic breaking" clacks from many short noise bursts
  (car bumps, dropped items). Use soft, low thuds and distance falloff, and scan the track for sharp spikes before rendering.
- A human moment lands well (the last man dying on a bench, counter 1 -> 0, heartbeat stopping).
