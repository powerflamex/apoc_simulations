# Next Short: "What if you stood on Jupiter?" (falling in) — approved plan, not built yet

`jupiter/`, all-new scene (no reused street scene). ~70 s, one continuous fall, depth counter racing from frame 0.
Check every number before it goes in a caption.

**First step when building:** make frame 0 and test stills before anything else. The clouds are the risk: if they
look mushy/hazy, frame 0 fails the hook rule. Strong colors, dark-blue sky above, sharp small Sun.

## What the viewer sees

- **0:00** You in a spacesuit, boots dangling over orange/white cloud bands; the Great Red Spot glowing on the
  horizon, dark-blue sky, small sharp Sun. Title + counter (DEPTH: 0 km) on screen and already moving.
- **0:01** You drop. "There's no ground. You start sinking."
- **0:04** Arms dragged down. "You weigh 2.5x more: 150 lb → 380 lb." (cloud-top gravity 2.53 g)
- **0:09** 350 mph wind slams you sideways, clouds streak; the Sun races across the sky and sets.
  "A day here lasts 10 hours." (9 h 56 min)
- **0:17** Into the white ammonia clouds, frost spreads over the visor. "-130°C. Colder than anywhere on Earth."
- **0:24** Brown clouds; daylight fades out behind you.
- **0:30** Storm layer (water clouds): lightning bursts all around, lighting cloud towers taller than mountains.
- **0:38** Pitch black. A small NASA probe sinks past, glowing and crumpling.
  "150 km down. NASA's Galileo probe died here." (Dec 1995, signal lost ~23 bar, ~150 °C)
- **0:46** The air itself glows dull red; your visor cracks. "The pressure of the deepest ocean on Earth." (~1,000 bar)
- **0:54** Glittering specks fall like hail. "Scientists think it may rain diamonds here." (a theory: say "may")
- **1:00** Gas turns into a glowing orange liquid with no surface. "The air turns into an ocean, but there's no surface."
- **1:06** Yellow-hot, then white-hot. "20,000 km down. Liquid metal, hotter than the Sun's surface."
- **1:10** Full white flash.
- **Loop** Cut inside the white back to the cloud tops; the white clears to frame 0.

Why it should hold viewers: something gets worse every few seconds (heavier → wind → cold → dark → lightning →
crush → glow → metal) and the counter promises a "bottom".

Backup idea if this one stalls: "What if Jupiter replaced the Moon?" (giant Jupiter 40x the Moon's width over a city;
37-hour "month"; 2-hour eclipse at noon every orbit; auroras; tides ~26,000x stronger → sea drains, wave, deep-water loop).
