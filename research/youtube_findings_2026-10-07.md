# YouTube research: what gets a Short past YouTube's first test (2026-10-07)

8 channels, 46 Shorts (stats in `youtube_2026-10-07.csv`). 14 downloaded on the owner's PC (YouTube blocks
downloads from the cloud) and studied frame by frame: openings (0-12 s), endings, loudness, cuts. Videos are not
kept in the repo.

Channels: @what-ifsimulation, @lunarcheeseee (Lunar), @RealityRenderedReal, @marmoushanimations,
@PixelHistorianAnim, @cloveranimationns (Clover), @fathom.animations.

## The ~1,000-view wall
- Every Short in the niche ends up either at ~1,000-2,200 views or stuck under ~500. None is past 2,222.
  Fathom has 13,000 subscribers and still gets ~1,200 per Short. YouTube shows a new Short to a first test group
  of about a thousand people; nobody in the niche has passed that test yet (ours: 1,100-1,600, same group).
- Lunar's "Earth stopped spinning" (20.4M on TikTok) has 25 views on YouTube after 10 h; the Moon crash (8.7M)
  has 21. The same clip, so the first push also depends on things outside the video (a new channel, several
  uploads in a day, reposts), not only on how good the video is.

## Same channel, same posting time, very different results
Best evidence: Shorts posted together by one channel, so the channel and timing are the same.

| Channel | Got the first push (~1,100-1,600) | Stalled (<320) |
|---|---|---|
| PixelHistorian | Volcano 1,159: frame 0 = dark ash cloud "THAT ISN'T SMOKE.", then a crowd and the volcano | Lightning 161 (same minute): frame 0 = almost all white, a faint bolt; then a nearly empty stormy rooftop |
| PixelHistorian | Sinkhole 1,275: a street full of people walking at you, the road moves at 5 s | Avalanche 190: white snow slope, tiny lift, low contrast, nothing happens for 12 s |
| PixelHistorian | Asteroid 1,642: sunny beach packed with people, the meteor streak already in the sky at frame 0 | Gravity doubled 311: dim empty street, a statement ("EARTH'S GRAVITY DOUBLES.") not a question, nothing until 8 s |
| RealityRendered | Solar flare 1,149: a giant glowing Sun over a lit city, "CME ARRIVAL 17:07:35" racing (10 h gone in 12 s) | All ice melted 49: "A normal day in a coastal city.", sea level +0 ft for 5 s |
| RealityRendered | Saturn's rings 1,132: huge rings across the sky on frame 0 | Gamma-ray burst 79: almost black lake at night, clock ticking in real time, "A perfect night at the lake." |
| What if Simulation | Moon crash 1,294 | Earth stopped rotating 82: faint white title on a hazy bright sky (hard to read); it was also the channel's first upload |

Also past the test: Fathom "space without a suit" 1,329 (Earth from orbit with aurora, air pressure racing
14.7 -> 3.1 psi in 3 s, the window blows out at 2 s); Marmoush "snowed for 1,000 years" 1,368 (busy street,
the snow piles up visibly within 2 s, Day 1 -> Week 7 in 12 s).

## What the ones that passed have in common
1. **Frame 0 reads at a glance:** strong contrast and colour. The flops open on an almost white frame (lightning,
   avalanche), an almost black one (gamma-ray), a dim one (gravity) or a hazy one with a faint title (Earth
   stopped).
2. **The unusual thing is already on screen, or visibly changing within ~2 s:** a meteor, a giant Sun, rings,
   an ash cloud, snow piling up, a window blowing out. The flops open on calm normality ("A normal day...",
   "A perfect night...") and make you wait 8-12 s.
3. **Counters race:** 10 hours in 12 s, psi dropping every frame, days flying by. The flops' counters sit still
   (+0 ft) or tick in real time (11:59:45 PM, 11:59:46 PM...).
4. **People in the scene:** crowds, a busy street, a packed beach. Three of the stalled ones have nobody visible.
5. **A question, not a statement,** in the title on screen.

Not a difference: length (52-100 s in both groups), cuts, loudness, endings (nearly all of them, hits and flops,
end on a dark end card).

## What this means for our videos
- Our recipe already covers 3-5 (racing counter, people, the question). The new rules are 1 and 2: frame 0 must
  be high-contrast and show something already unusual or moving, not a calm "before" scene.
- Black hole (posting next): opens on a calm evening sky with a racing countdown; the hole shows at ~13 s. Like
  sun-vanishes (normal scene + racing countdown, passed), so probably fine, but it is the weakest kind of opening.
- New videos: put the strange thing in the very first frame (e.g. "Earth spun faster": the Sun already
  streaking across the sky at frame 0, not a normal afternoon).
