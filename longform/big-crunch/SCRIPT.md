# Big Crunch voiceover: read this out loud

**How to record (this time, from what we learned on the Big Rip):**
- Phone voice recorder, airplane mode. **No sock and no blanket** this time (they made it sound muffled). Stand in a
  closet with clothes hanging around you, or a small room with a bed and curtains. Fans and AC off if you can.
- Hold the phone at your cheek, a hand's width away, beside the corner of your mouth (not straight in front).
- **One file, start to finish.** Mess up? Stop, breathe, and say that whole sentence again from its start. Don't
  restart the file; I find every retake and keep your last one.
- A short breath between lines. Where it says **[wait 3 seconds]**, stay quiet for 3 seconds.
- Talk like you're telling a friend something crazy. Slow is good. Speed doesn't matter; I place every line at its own
  moment in the video.
- When you're done, put the file in this folder (`longform/big-crunch/`) or just tell me where it is.

### 1. The opening

In seven minutes, the whole universe is going to fall in on you.

### 2. The promenade

This is the Big Crunch.

Right now, the universe is growing. Every galaxy is flying away from every other galaxy.

But in 2025, scientists looked at new measurements, and found it might not last.

The growing is slowing down.

In about eleven billion years, it could stop. And then, everything starts falling back in.

Their guess for the end: about twenty billion years from now.

Our Sun will be long gone by then.

But imagine Earth is still here, just like tonight.

And imagine you could stand right here, and watch the whole thing.

### 3. Andromeda

The first thing you'd notice isn't the crunch.

It's Andromeda.

Our biggest neighbor galaxy is already falling toward us.

Every night, it's a little bigger.

Then it fills half the sky.

In the next few billion years, it crashes right into the Milky Way.

Hundreds of billions of stars, pouring through each other.

And almost none of them actually hit.

**[wait 3 seconds]**

The two galaxies just melt into one giant ball of stars.

### 4. The universe stops growing

Nine billion years left.

This is where everything changes.

Point a telescope at any far-off galaxy. For billions of years, its light has looked red.

That's what light does when something is flying away from you.

Now, slowly, it turns white.

Then blue.

Blue means it's coming back.

The universe has stopped growing. And every galaxy out there is now falling toward us.

### 5. The galaxies come back

A billion years left.

The night sky starts filling up.

Galaxies everywhere. Bigger every night. Brighter.

A hundred million years left, they crash into each other. All of them.

**[wait 3 seconds]**

There's no more dark space left in the sky.

### 6. The night gets warm

And now, something strange happens to the night.

Space has always been freezing. Minus two hundred and seventy degrees.

That's the leftover heat of the Big Bang, spread out so thin it's almost nothing.

But now space is getting smaller. And that old heat is getting squeezed back together.

Sixteen million years left, the night sky is warmer than ice.

Fifteen million years left, it's as warm as a summer evening.

You'd never need a jacket again.

And it doesn't stop.

Ten million years left, the sky is hotter than boiling water.

The oceans boil away.

Not because of the Sun. Because of the sky.

### 7. The sky glows

Three million years left.

The night sky starts to glow.

Dull red. Like the inside of an oven.

And it's the same in every direction. There's nowhere to hide from it.

### 8. The ground melts

One million years left. The sky is over a thousand degrees.

The ground starts to glow. Then it melts.

The city goes first. Then the rock under it.

Imagine you're somehow still here, floating over a sea of lava.

### 9. The fog of fire

Four hundred thousand years left.

The sky is so hot, it starts tearing atoms apart.

And you can't see the galaxies anymore. You can't see anything.

The whole universe turns into a fog of fire.

The last time it looked like this was right after the Big Bang.

### 10. The stars

A hundred thousand years left.

The sky is now hotter than the surface of the Sun.

Look for the Sun.

It's gone. Not because it died. Because the sky around it is just as bright.

Stars can't get rid of their heat anymore. So they boil away, from the outside in.

Earth goes the same way.

**[wait 3 seconds]**

### 11. The last three minutes

Three minutes left.

Right after the Big Bang, it took about three minutes for the centers of the first atoms to form.

Now it happens backwards.

The center of every atom gets smashed apart.

Every atom in you. Every atom in Earth. Every atom that ever existed.

### 12. The end

One hundred-thousandth of a second left.

Even the pieces of atoms melt, into a soup of the tiniest things there are.

And all of space, everything that ever existed, squeezes into one point.

**[wait 3 seconds]**

And then, maybe, it bursts out again.

A new Big Bang.

### 13. The ending

That's the Big Crunch.

One study says it's about twenty billion years away.

But a lot of scientists think the universe will never crunch. That it'll keep growing forever, and slowly go cold and dark.

That ending is even stranger. And it's the one I'm simulating next.

So, which ending would you rather live through? Tell me in the comments.
