# Long video: "What if your IQ was 1,000 times higher?"

16:9 (1920x1080, 30 fps), about 6.5 minutes, narrated by the owner (script: `longform/iq-1000/SCRIPT.md`).
A new project and a new scene: a busy city plaza on a summer afternoon with a storm coming in (`longform/iq-1000/`;
only the shared building blocks are reused: people, cars, the render and compositor code, the counter style).

## Topic check (YouTube, Oct 22)
The topic is hot: Manifesting "What if your IQ was 10x higher?" 742K in 8 days; IFopposite "What If You Had 1000 IQ?"
24K (7 d) and "Infinite IQ" 120K (6 d); BrainBracket "IQ was 1,000" 2.8K (2 d); a wave channel's "IQ Increased And
Couldn't Stop?" 3.2K (3 h old). All of them are cartoon stories (you predict everything, solve problems, get
recruited). Nobody shows what a faster brain would actually *look like* from where you stand. Our angle: the
real-world consequence of a brain that runs faster, the world slowing down around you, as a first-person sim with a
racing counter. The owner's rule: no "...couldn't stop?" in the title.

## The idea in one line
Your IQ keeps doubling, and your brain runs twice as fast each time. So the world around you slows down: people
walk underwater, rain falls like snow, then hangs in the air, the sound sinks out of hearing, and you watch a
lightning bolt crawl down the sky. At 1,000x a second lasts 17 minutes. The catch: your brain is cooking.

## The science (numbers we can say out loud)
- IQ is a ranking: average 100, every 15 points is one step (standard deviation) rarer. 145 is 1 in 1,000. Nobody
  knows Einstein's IQ (he never took a test); the usual guess is 160 (1 in ~30,000). **195 is about 1 in 8 billion:
  the smartest person on Earth.** Past that the scale stops meaning anything, so the video counts brain speed.
- Brain speed x thinking: 2x faster = a second feels like 2. At 1,024x a second feels like 17 minutes.
- What slows down (world speed = 1 / brain speed): a walker (1.4 m/s); a car at 30 mph (13 m/s) moves at walking
  pace at ~9x; rain (9 m/s) falls like snow at ~16x, hangs (7 cm/s) at 128x; a pigeon's wingbeat (~6 a second) takes
  ~3 s at 16x; a guitar's low string (82 Hz) visibly swings 2-3 times a second at 32x.
- Sound: every pitch drops by the same factor (you hear the world slowed down). Speech (100-4,000 Hz) is a rumble at
  ~32x and under 20 Hz (below hearing) at ~128-200x: by ~130x the city is silent.
- Your body doesn't speed up: a word (~0.6 s) takes 15 s to say at 24x; moving your eyes (~0.05-0.2 s) takes
  seconds. Your heart (~70 a minute) beats once every ~2 minutes at 128x, once every **14 minutes at 1,024x**.
- Lightning: the stepped leader takes ~30 ms to come down, so at ~280x it crawls down the sky for ~8 s; the return
  stroke is a flash; a bolt has ~3-4 strokes ~40 ms apart (seen as flashes every ~15 s at 300-500x); the thunder (1 km
  away, 3 s later) never arrives until you slow back down.
- A soap bubble's film tears at ~10 m/s: a 30 cm bubble pops in ~30 ms = 30 s at 1,024x.
- The cost: the brain runs on ~20 W (a dim bulb). At the same efficiency, 1,000x the thinking = ~20 kW (10 space
  heaters). Blood carries away ~20 W, so a 1.4 kg brain (3.6 kJ/kg/°C) heats at ~0.24 °C per *felt* minute: 37 °C ->
  ~38.2 °C by the end of the climb; ~42 °C (it starts to cook) in **16 more felt minutes, under one real second**.
- The whole video, start of the climb to the end, is about **one minute** of real time (the climb itself takes
  43 s: each 30 s step lasts half as long in real time as the one before; computed in `timeline.js`, `worldT`).

## Build
- `longform/iq-1000/`: the long-video engine from `longform/big-crunch/` (render.mjs, compose.js with an extra canvas
  for the thought lines, overlay, voice slots, readalong.mjs), 16:9 only; a new scene.
- The world is a pure function of **world time** `worldT(v)` (real seconds), which slows as the brain speeds up;
  the camera, the thought lines and the captions run on the video clock. The city's sound is made in real time and
  played back along `worldT`, so it slows and sinks in pitch by itself, like a tape slowing down.
- Captions/counter timed to the script first; the recording drops in when the owner sends it.
