# Long video: "What if space ripped apart? You'd have 30 minutes left."

16:9 (1920x1080, 30 fps), about 7 minutes, narrated by the owner. A new project and a new scene (nothing reused).
Topic checked on YouTube Oct 18 (see `research/longform_wave_2026-10-18.md`): the Big Rip has only old explainers and
tiny uploads; nobody has done it as a "you are standing there" sim, and none of the clone channels has touched it.

## The idea in one line
Space is stretching, and it speeds up forever. The stretch gets stronger than everything holding things together,
biggest first: the galaxy, the stars, the planets, the Sun, the Moon, Earth, and last, you.

## The science (numbers we can say out loud)
- Caldwell, Kamionkowski & Weinberg (2003), "Phantom Energy and Cosmic Doomsday": with their example dark energy the
  Big Rip comes in about 22 billion years. Before the end: the Milky Way is torn apart **60 million years** before,
  the solar system **3 months** before, **Earth explodes 30 minutes** before, atoms **10^-19 s** before.
- Everything else follows the same rule (a thing comes loose about 0.28 x its orbit time before the end, for their
  example): Neptune ~46 years, Saturn ~8 years, Jupiter ~3 years, Mars ~6 months, Earth from the Sun ~3 months,
  the Moon ~1 week, TV satellites ~6.7 hours, GPS ~3.4 hours.
- After Earth comes loose, the gap to the Sun keeps growing faster and faster: with 1 month left the Sun looks about
  5x smaller, with 1 week left it is a bright dot.
- Phones keep working (cell towers are on the ground); what goes dark when satellites leave is satellite TV and GPS.
- Honest ending: newer measurements (DESI, 2024-25) hint dark energy may be getting weaker, which would mean no Big
  Rip. The video says "it might never happen".
- The "you watch it all" part is the same make-believe as trillion-years: the narration says "imagine".

## Narration script (about 570 words, ~4.5 minutes of talking at a slow, dramatic pace; the rest is picture and sound)
Read it like you're telling a friend something crazy. `/` = a short breath. `[silence]` = stop and wait 3 seconds
(that's where I put the big sound). Don't worry about speed; I place every sentence at its own moment.

**0:00 (flash-forward)**
In seven minutes, / space itself is going to rip you apart.

**0:04 (the city)**
This is the Big Rip. / Space has been stretching ever since the Big Bang. / Every second, the gap between galaxies
gets a little wider. / And for the last five billion years, / it's been speeding up. / Nobody knows what's pushing it.
Scientists just call it dark energy. / Now imagine it never stops speeding up. / And imagine you could stand right
here, / and watch what happens.

**0:30 (the Milky Way)**
Sixty million years before the end, / the stretching gets stronger than the gravity holding our galaxy together. /
The Milky Way comes apart. / Two hundred billion stars, / flung out into the dark. / Look up. / You can see the band
of light across the sky fray at the edges, / and start to unravel.
[silence]
From down here, / it's the most beautiful thing anyone has ever seen. / It's also a warning. / Because the
stretching only gets stronger. / And everything smaller is next.

**1:10 (the stars go out)**
One by one, / the stars slide away from each other. / They get dimmer. / Redder. / Then they're gone. / A few
thousand years before the end, / the night sky is black. / No stars. / Not one. / The only lights left up there are
the Moon, / and a few planets. / Our neighbors. / For now.

**1:40 (the planets)**
Eight years left. / Saturn drifts out of its orbit. / Nobody down here notices. / Three years left. / Jupiter, / one
of the brightest lights in the night sky, / starts to fade. / Every night it's a little smaller. / Then one night you
look up, / and it isn't there. / Six months left. / Mars goes the same way. / And then, / it's our turn.

**2:30 (the Sun)**
Three months before the end, / the space between Earth and the Sun starts stretching faster than the Sun can hold
on to us. / We come loose. / At first, nothing seems wrong. / It's a normal day. / But every morning, / the Sun comes
up a little smaller. / A little colder. / With a month left, / it looks five times smaller than it should. / It's the
middle of summer, / and it's snowing. / Noon looks like sunset. / Then like night. / By the last week, / the Sun is
just a bright star in a black sky.
And then, / the Moon goes.

**3:40 (the Moon)**
For four and a half billion years, / the Moon has hung over us. / Now, / every night, / it's smaller. / Further away. /
On the last night you can see it, / it's a dot.
[silence]
Then it's gone too. / We're alone now. / Just Earth, / drifting in the dark.

**4:30 (satellites)**
Six hours left. / The satellites go. / One after another, / they drift off into space. / Satellite TV goes dark. /
Then GPS. / Every map on every phone stops knowing where you are. / Everyone is looking at their screen. / Nobody
knows what to do.

**5:00 (Earth splits)**
Thirty minutes. / Now the stretching is stronger than Earth's own gravity. / The air goes first. / It starts
drifting up. / Off the streets. / Off the planet. / Then, / the ground.
[silence]
Earth comes apart, / like it was never stuck together. / And somehow, / you're still here. / Floating in the
pieces.

**5:50 (you)**
Now there's nothing left big enough to break. / Except you. / In the last fraction of a second, / the space
inside your body starts to stretch. / Your cells come apart. / Then your molecules. / And one ten-billionth of a
billionth of a second before the end, / your atoms.
[silence]

**6:40 (white, back to the city)**
That's the Big Rip. / It might be twenty-two billion years away. / Some new measurements even hint it might never
happen. / Scientists still don't know. / So, / which ending of the universe should I simulate next? / Tell me in the
comments.

## Recording (owner)
- Phone, quiet small room with soft stuff (a closet of clothes or under a blanket is great), phone a hand's width
  from your mouth, slightly to the side. Fans and AC off if you can; a steady hum is fine, I can remove it.
- One file, start to finish. **Mess up? Stop, breathe, and say the whole sentence again** from its start. Keep going;
  don't restart the file. I find every retake and keep the last good one.
- Leave a short breath between sentences (the `/`), so I can cut cleanly.
- Speed doesn't matter much. Talk naturally.
- Send the file (voice memo is fine). Then I cut it, clean it, place each line on the timeline and lower the music
  under it.

## How I'll fix the recording
- Transcribe it on the PC (faster-whisper, word by word with times), match it to the script, keep the last take of
  each sentence, cut out "uh"s and long gaps.
- Steady noise (fan, AC, hum, PC): removed with ffmpeg noise reduction. Sudden noises over a word (a dog, a door,
  someone talking): can't be removed cleanly; I'll list those sentences for a quick re-record.
- Too fast: each sentence gets placed at its own moment, so the pauses between them stretch to fit; a sentence itself
  can be slowed ~5-10% without sounding off.
- A wrong word that wasn't re-said: I can sometimes swap in the same word from elsewhere in the recording; otherwise
  it goes on the re-record list (one short file with just those lines).
- Room echo is the one thing that can't really be fixed afterwards, which is why the closet.
