# Big Rip voiceover: read this out loud

**How to record (2 minutes of setup):**
- Use your phone's voice recorder, phone on airplane mode. Blanket over your head (and over the laptop screen, with
  the laptop as far away as you can). Turn off fans and AC if you can.
- Thin sock over the bottom of the phone. Hold it a hand's width away, at your cheek / the corner of your mouth,
  not straight in front (so the "p" and "b" puffs miss it).
- Say a test line first and send it to me: "The planet broke apart, and big pieces pounded the beach."
- Record **one file, start to finish.** Mess up? Stop, breathe, and say that whole sentence again from its start.
  Don't restart the file; I find every retake and keep your last one.
- Take a short breath between lines. Where it says **[wait 3 seconds]**, stay quiet for 3 seconds.
- Talk like you're telling a friend something crazy. Slow is good. Speed doesn't matter; I place every line at its own
  moment in the video.
- When you're done, put the file in this folder (`longform/big-rip/`) or just tell me where it is.

### 1. The opening

In seven minutes, space itself is going to rip you apart.

### 2. The city

This is the Big Rip.

Space has been stretching ever since the Big Bang.

Every second, the gap between galaxies gets a little wider.

And for the last five billion years, it's been speeding up.

Nobody knows what's pushing it. Scientists just call it dark energy.

Now imagine it never stops speeding up.

And imagine you could stand right here, and watch what happens.

### 3. The Milky Way

Sixty million years before the end, the stretching gets stronger than the gravity holding our galaxy together.

The Milky Way comes apart.

Two hundred billion stars, flung out into the dark.

Look up.

You can see the band of light across the sky fray at the edges, and start to unravel.

**[wait 3 seconds]**

From down here, it's the most beautiful thing anyone has ever seen.

It's also a warning.

Because the stretching only gets stronger.

And everything smaller is next.

### 4. The stars go out

One by one, the stars slide away from each other.

They get dimmer. Redder. Then they're gone.

A few thousand years before the end, the night sky is black.

No stars. Not one.

The only lights left up there are the Moon, and a few planets.

Our neighbors. For now.

### 5. The planets

Eight years left.

Saturn drifts out of its orbit. Nobody down here notices.

Three years left.

Jupiter, one of the brightest lights in the night sky, starts to fade.

Every night it's a little smaller.

Then one night you look up, and it isn't there.

Six months left.

Mars goes the same way.

And then, it's our turn.

### 6. The Sun

Three months before the end, the space between Earth and the Sun starts stretching faster than the Sun can hold on to us.

We come loose.

At first, nothing seems wrong. It's a normal day.

But every morning, the Sun comes up a little smaller. A little colder.

With a month left, it looks five times smaller than it should.

It's the middle of summer, and it's snowing.

Noon looks like sunset. Then like night.

By the last week, the Sun is just a bright star in a black sky.

And then, the Moon goes.

### 7. The Moon

For four and a half billion years, the Moon has hung over us.

Now, every night, it's smaller. Further away.

On the last night you can see it, it's a dot.

**[wait 3 seconds]**

Then it's gone too.

We're alone now. Just Earth, drifting in the dark.

### 8. The satellites

Six hours left.

The satellites go. One after another, they drift off into space.

Satellite TV goes dark. Then GPS.

Every map on every phone stops knowing where you are.

Everyone is looking at their screen. Nobody knows what to do.

### 9. Earth splits

Thirty minutes.

Now the stretching is stronger than Earth's own gravity.

The air goes first.

It starts drifting up. Off the streets. Off the planet.

Then, the ground.

**[wait 3 seconds]**

Earth comes apart, like it was never stuck together.

And somehow, you're still here. Floating in the pieces.

### 10. You

Now there's nothing left big enough to break.

Except you.

In the last fraction of a second, the space inside your body starts to stretch.

Your cells come apart. Then your molecules.

And one ten-billionth of a billionth of a second before the end, your atoms.

**[wait 3 seconds]**

### 11. The ending

That's the Big Rip.

It might be twenty-two billion years away.

Some new measurements even hint it might never happen. Scientists still don't know.

So, which ending of the universe should I simulate next? Tell me in the comments.
