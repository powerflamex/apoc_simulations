## The science (numbers we can say out loud)
- Luu, Qiu & Tye (2025), "The Lifespan of our Universe" (JCAP; Cornell news): fitting the DES/DESI dark-energy
  data, the universe reaches its biggest size about **11 billion years from now**, then falls back in; the end comes
  **about 20 billion years from now** (a lifespan of 33 billion years). It's one model: the video says "might".
- Andromeda is falling toward us and should crash into the Milky Way in the next several billion years (new studies
  put it at about a coin flip within 10 billion; the video says "in the next few billion years"). Stars almost never
  hit each other; the two galaxies mix into one big ball of stars.
- After the turnaround, distant galaxies' light goes from red (flying away) to blue (coming back).
- The end of a crunch looks like the start of the Big Bang played backwards (Rees 1969, "The collapse of the
  universe"). So the time left at each temperature of the sky is about the age of the universe when it was that hot:
  - the night sky (the Big Bang's leftover light) passes 0 °C ~16 million years before the end, room temperature
    ~15 million, 100 °C (the oceans boil, heated by the sky, not the Sun) ~10 million;
    (time left at a sky temperature from the matter + radiation age formula, t = 17.24 Gyr x
    [(a - 2a_eq) sqrt(a + a_eq) + 2 a_eq^1.5], a_eq = 1/3400, T = 2.725 K / a: the same numbers drive the counter)
  - glows dull red (~500 °C) ~3 million; over 1,000 °C (rock melts) ~1 million;
  - ~3,000 °C ~400,000 years before: atoms are torn apart and space turns into a glowing fog you can't see through
    (the reverse of the moment the universe first turned clear);
  - hotter than the Sun's surface (5,500 °C) ~100,000 years before: the Sun is lost in a sky as bright as it is, and
    stars can't get rid of their heat, so they boil away from the outside in (Rees);
  - the last ~3 minutes: atom cores (nuclei) are smashed apart (the "first three minutes" in reverse), ~1 billion °C;
  - the last ~10 millionths of a second: protons and neutrons melt into quarks (~2 trillion °C).
- Honest framing, as in the Big Rip: our Sun dies long before (7.6 billion years from now), so the narration says
  "imagine Earth is still here". The closing says many scientists expect the universe to grow forever instead.
